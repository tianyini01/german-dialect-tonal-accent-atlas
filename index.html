DATA GUIDE

Reading the contours

Included F0 data can be analyzed immediately. You can open a compatible CSV in your browser to explore additional measurements.

F0 CSV

One row per time position. Give elapsed time from the vowel onset in milliseconds and F0 in hertz. Blank F0 values represent missing or unvoiced points.

speaker_id, pair_id, word, context_id, condition, token_id, time_ms, duration_ms, f0_hzBlank F0 template

How F0 is compared

The default horizontal axis uses elapsed milliseconds. The optional normalized axis shows each token's progress from 0 to 100% of its measured duration. This rescales time only: F0 stays in Hz. Group means are recalculated in 10 ms or 10 percentage-point bins from observed samples, without filling missing points or inventing endpoints.

F0 comparisons use one recorded context at a time. In Speakers mode, each speaker has a separate panel with both conditions of the selected minimal pair. The panels share the same time and F0 scales. Sparse mean points are hidden by the display cutoff, which can be set to 1. The normalized axis hides duration differences, so read the measured duration shown with the chart. In Datasets mode, each source contributes both conditions from one complete minimal pair, with a separate context choice for each source. Leer vowel length and Aachen historical accent classes are different designs; the four curves are a visual comparison, not a pooled effect or GAMM significance test.

Etymology word pairs

Candidate lexical alignments open two separate dialect panels. Each speaker selector can show one recorded ID or the mean across all IDs with both words in the selected context. F0 means first average within each speaker and then across speakers in each time bin. The duration mean gives each speaker or workbook series equal weight; dots and medians remain token-level measurements. One context choice maps sentence type and position for Leer and Aachen, though Aachen does not record the same focus detail. Baden has only one workbook context, whose prosodic relationship to Leer is unverified; the choice filters Leer alone. Local condition names and measurements are not pooled across dialects.

The Leer study word list gives the Leer glosses. The Aachen CSV does not identify which sense of Reis was elicited; the rice and twig entries are distinct. The historical risi entry supports the giant-word link.

Keeping context visible follows the comparison design in Gussenhoven & Peters (2004); Peters (2006) reports position-dependent duration differences in Cologne.

Physical-time GAMM (new refit)

This exploratory model is newly fitted from the included measured F0 tables. Its horizontal axis is elapsed vowel time in milliseconds; F0 is fitted in log Hz and displayed in Hz. Each condition curve stops at that context and condition's observed median vowel duration, so longer vowels occupy more horizontal space. The model does not predict vowel duration.

The fit pools the included speakers and word pairs using random intercepts and accounts for within-token correlation. Speaker and pair selectors do not refit this population view. Shading shows approximate pointwise 95% intervals, not a significance test. See the model specification and source hash beneath the chart.

Original GAMM view

Leer and Aachen also have precomputed predictions from their original research GAMMs. Choose Original GAMM (normalized) to see condition curves for the selected context. The model pools its source speakers and word pairs; the current speaker and pair choices do not change its curves. These models use normalized vowel time (0–100%) and semitones: Leer is relative to 100 Hz; Aachen is centered on each speaker's median. They cannot be placed on the measured Hz / actual-ms chart.

Each model panel includes a separate descriptive vowel-duration summary in milliseconds from the physical-time site table, across its speakers and pairs for that context. The GAMM itself does not model duration. Shading shows approximate pointwise 95% intervals, not a significance test. The browser does not refit these models for word pairs, speakers, or uploads. Leer model input has 480 contours; the physical-time site table has 479 with verified boundaries.

Included research data

Approved derived measurements are included for Leer and Aachen F0, and Leer, Baden and Cologne vowel duration. The atlas markers show approximate study areas; they are not speaker locations or dialect distribution boundaries.

Original recordings, TextGrid annotations and fieldwork workbooks are not included. A compatible CSV opened from your device remains in this browser session and is not uploaded to the site.

Reading duration results

Each dot is one measured vowel duration in milliseconds. The thick line marks a group's mean; the table also shows its median, range, token count, and contributing speaker or source-series IDs. Choose one recorded minimal pair for Leer or Baden; its two words remain bound to their conditions. Cologne compares F and NF for one recorded word at a time, without calling two different words a minimal pair. A dataset comparison keeps both conditions from each source. These are descriptive values, with no pooled effect or p-value.

Map and data scope

Atlas markers locate study areas approximately. They do not indicate individual speakers or dialect distribution boundaries.
